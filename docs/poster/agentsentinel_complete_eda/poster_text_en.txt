01

Introduction & objectives

Tool errors can recover; normal returns can still violate the task. Using only current and past observable events,
the study updates final-failure risk after each tool return and evaluates prediction, alert timing, cost and success
protection together. Can early stopping reduce wasted work without sacrificing recoverable runs? Prediction,
pre-violation warning and loss-free stopping are distinct objectives, not interchangeable outcomes.

03

Results / Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

TRAIN ONLY: 3,542 valid tasks and 27,367 prefixes; quality adds 58 invalid attempts. No validation/test EDA.
All 22 features: zero missing, negative or non-finite values; unique ID/step keys; no duplicate rows in 28,202 events.
4,702 genuine same-argument retries are not log duplicates. 62 time and 31 token outliers audited (6 tasks/24 files), retained.

A | Failure evidence and task heterogeneity (Fig. 1)

The 972 failures partition into violation+missing 411, violation-only 253, missing-only 277 and state-only 31.
757/972 (77.88%) exhausted 12 steps; 664 contained violations (overlapping evidence). All 31 state failures were order errors.
Template failure spans 0.67%–90.41%; two incident families contribute 67.70%. Random prefix splits would leak structure.

B | Assigned condition × workflow (Fig. 2)

Normal conditions still produced 170 failures, 159 with violations; faults are not necessary for failure.
Partial-response failure: incident 45.9% vs ticket 8.7%. Budget failures form 91.74%/94.47% of rate-limit/timeout failures.
Conditions are assigned, not guaranteed triggers. These are descriptive strata, not a universal causal hazard ranking.

C | Large full-run resource gaps, weak early separation (Fig. 3)

Median tokens, success/failure: incident 14,407/24,945.5; ticket 12,410/24,061.5.
Median seconds: 67.37/92.61 and 56.90/86.52; median tool steps: 7/12 and 6/12.
Yet step-3 token means span only 4,226–4,289 across four groups, before any applied violation.
Full-trajectory gaps do not prove early separability. Condition rankings differ across tokens, steps and time.

03

Results / EDA (continued)

D | Error recovery and completion timing (Fig. 4)

Among 2,570 successes, 1,316 (51.21%) had explicit tool errors and 548 (21.32%) had ≥3 errors.
Read errors recovered in 4,031/5,926 events (68.02%; median 1 step); incomplete policies completed in 590/804 (73.38%; 2 steps).
Recovery means a later same-tool/same-argument normal return or completed fields—not task success; follow-up is budget-limited.
77 successes first completed at step 12; 80 eventual failures once completed, then deteriorated after extra writes.

E | Early thresholds and correlation structure (Fig. 5)

Step-3 error × repetition has no consistent monotonic relation: incident 0-error/0-repeat failure is 42.8%,
versus 15.8% with repetition; a 50% ticket cell has n=2. This does not show repetition is protective.
Spearman: retry/repetition 0.981; tokens/cost 0.884; tokens/reasoning 0.894; output/reasoning 0.999/0.998 by workflow.
Undefined constant-column correlations are not zero. Correlated coefficients are not independent causal effects.

F | Cross-step trends, survivors and batch confounding (Fig. 6)

Eligible incident successes fall from 1,003 early to 84 at step 11, while 705 failures remain: populations change.
Report surviving n and common cohorts; do not confuse survivor composition with new information or monotonic gains.
Invalid rates at 50/10 workers are 8.00%/1.03%, confounded by date, restart phase and provider state; simulated timeout ≠ network wait.

Prediction & intervention / frozen main experiment

22 observable features: progress/writes, errors/retries, tokens/cost, time, recent window and tool context; LR C=1, unweighted.
At most 6 evenly spaced prefixes/task give 19,094 fit rows; three-fold parent-family CV; fold-only preprocessing.
KNN k=9 distance-weighted; fixed-OR, step and never-stop baselines. Validation selects lowest cost within a 5% false-stop budget.
Main probability threshold: 0.7380596938. Scoring follows tool return; it cannot undo the just-completed write.

05

Discussion, conclusion & limitations

Conclusion: runtime signals support risk monitoring and reduce some later waste.
Loss-free stopping, violation prevention and online gains remain unproven. Limits: one LLM, two simulated workflows;
six train/two test families; acceptance omits reading adequacy/final-reply quality; all outcomes are offline replay.
Next: independent families, preregistered protection, richer acceptance, warnings/deferred decisions/completion detection.
